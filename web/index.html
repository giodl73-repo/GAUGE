What do the source labels establish?

Twelve retained frequency quantities need original sources. One broad source description does not retain timetable bytes, exact article revisions or route/date/segment/day-specific locators. Source-needed does not establish that a value is wrong. Exact derivative-file hashes do not verify original timetable observations.

The daily averages are model inputs. They do not measure first/last departures, service span, spacing, operating days or whole-endpoint coverage. The Sunset Limited 0.4/day claim is rounded, not exactly 3/7 or proof of daily operation. Both the rounded value and the old three-per-week description need a dated source. Brand entries cannot be generalized to every segment or a representative national network.

What remains unassessed

Trip time, reliability, ridership, modal shift, costs, host-track capacity, access equity and the other DIM-01..13 dimensions remain unassessed here. Adequate frequency alone is not a full service promise, a build recommendation or a national finding.

The historical baseline preserves committed scores and counts from the source-needed historical model corpus. Every edited corridor changes in your hypothetical scenario. Unedited corridors retain their historical values. The bar applies to both runs. Rust uses GAUGE's existing gap detector: the bottom quartile must fall below the bar to trigger a dispersion signal; at least half of scored corridors below the bar is classified as systemic, otherwise as a concentrated tail. “No frequency dispersion gap” refers only to this dimension.
